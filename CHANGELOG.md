# Changelog

## Unreleased

### Improvements
- Agents can capture, read and record a covered window or one on another workspace without moving your focus, and `agent list windows` lists every workspace's windows with `visible`.
- Agent captures no longer show a card or notification; agents can ask for one with `--ui`, and `--out` now writes an extra copy while the capture still goes to the library.

## 0.2.0 - 2026-11-12T02:13:11+06:00

### New
- Share a link to a capture from its card, the editor (Share in its title bar or the Share Image key) or the trim viewer (Share in its title bar or the Share Video key, sending the clip as trimmed, with your export choices): a popover asks for a title and, on easyshot Free, 1, 3 or 7 days (Enter shares), with no first-share step, the link is copied as the upload starts, and a toast shows progress and Cancel, then Open and Copy Link.
- easyshot Free needs no account: images up to 10 MB, GIFs up to 20 MB and videos up to 200 MB (a bigger video is re-encoded to fit); the file is deleted when the days end and the link then shows only "Page not found".
- Shares go to your own S3-compatible bucket once its endpoint, bucket and public URL are filled in under Settings › Sharing, otherwise to easyshot Free; the page says which, holds the bucket's keys (in your keyring, or a private file) and tests the connection. A bucket whose keys are missing fails the share instead of using Free.
- Shared links in the gallery: a cloud mark with the time left, the bucket or Expired; Copy Link, Open Link, Copy File Link and Delete Cloud Share in the item menu; Remove for an expired link; and a Cloud Shares section that keeps every link, even when its file is gone. Cards with a live link carry the mark too.
- `easyshot share FILE [--days 1|3|7] [--title TEXT]` uploads an image, a video or a recording and prints its link.
- The share page fits your screen, showing a very tall image from its top, and View full size (or a click on the image) opens an image across the whole window.

### Bug fixes
- Copy on a recording's card now puts the video on the clipboard as an MP4 file, ready to paste into a file manager or chat app; before, it did nothing.

## 0.1.0 - 2026-11-11T05:25:23+06:00

### New
- Screenshots of a region, a window, a whole screen or the previous target, always on a frozen screen; text and QR codes (OCR); a colour picker; pins that stay on top; an editor with 13 tools; and a history of your captures.
- Scrolling capture: draw a region (Space picks a window instead; Shift-click the bar's Scroll button or `capture scroll --window` starts on the window picker), scroll, and get one tall image, with the page's fixed top and bottom bars shown once and a side strip that does not scroll, such as a minimap, kept as the first view showed it, while page content reaching into it stays whole; a scrollbar at the right edge is cut off the image; pages with a playing video or animation join too; scrolling by hand works wherever the pointer is, and Auto Scroll keeps the pointer and hover effects out of the image.
- `capture previous` repeats the last area, display or window; a window that has closed gets a toast, not a capture.
- `--timed [N]` on `capture region`, `window` and `fullscreen`, and a timer button on the capture bar: pick first, count down, then take a fresh frame.
- One capture runs at a time and nothing waits in line: while you pick, another capture key switches the picker to that mode on the same frozen screen and the same key closes it; once a capture is under way, a new one is refused with a toast "A capture is in progress"; screenshots still work during a recording, and an agent asking during a capture is told busy.
- The capture bar never locks your keyboard: you can click another app and type while it is open, Esc closes it while it has focus, and opening it again from its key, the tray or `easyshot capture-bar` closes it. The recording control bar never takes the keyboard.
- Open any image or video from your computer: the Home window's Open… button, dropping a file onto the window, Open with Easyshot in your file manager, or `easyshot open FILE`; a PNG or JPEG opens in the editor, an MP4, WebM, MKV, MOV or GIF in the trim viewer, and any other file shows a toast.
- The editor's inspector on the right, with a button in the tool row to hide it, and its Background, Camera and Progressive Blur sections: colours, 24 gradients and your own (two to four colours at any angle, made from the selected one and kept in `gradients.json`), seven built-in wallpapers or your own, padding, shadow, corners, a canvas shape (Free, 1:1, 4:3, 3:2, 16:9) that appears once there is a background, alignment, and a focus pad for the sharp area; while you drag a camera or blur slider the picture stays sharp, as it will export.
- Each editor tool's options show in the inspector, with a back arrow to the image settings, and stay until you change tools: colour and stroke, arrowheads and line style for arrows, font (the bundled fonts and every installed one, searchable; a character the font lacks, such as Bengali, Arabic or Chinese, draws in an installed font that has it, in text and in the watermark), size, bold, italic and underline for text, blur strength, the Highlighter's mode, and crop's ratio with Reset, Cancel and Apply.
- Each editor tool's pointer shows what a press makes: a cross with a small rectangle, circle, line or arrow for those shapes, a pen for drawing, a text cursor for text, a badge for the counter, a dotted square for Highlight and an eraser for Pixelate and Blur.
- The Highlighter tool (key M) paints translucent colour that keeps the text under it readable, freehand or as a straight line (Shift snaps to 15 degrees; M again switches mode).
- Smart Redaction scans the image for private text and covers it, flashes what it found, and says what it hid ("Hid 3 items: emails, keys") with an Undo button, and the message waits while the pointer is on it; Pixelate and Blur keep a minimum strength tied to the text height, so covered text cannot be read back.
- Crop, rotate and flip the image in the editor; annotations follow, and each step can be undone.
- In the editor the mouse wheel zooms about the pointer and touchpad scrolling pans; annotations stay on the image.
- Hold Space and drag an annotation to move it, with any tool; dragging empty canvas pans.
- Text can have a background and a border.
- New text starts at size 24, any size from 4 to 300 can be typed in the Text options, and a dashed box shows where it goes while you type.
- Esc steps back one level: it commits the text you are typing, leaves crop, clears the selection, then switches to the Select tool.
- The editor remembers your last annotation style across sessions, and each tool's options have a Reset to defaults button.
- Very large screenshots open in the editor with a low-resolution preview that keeps it fast, while saving, exporting and copying use the full image; Settings › General › Always use a low-resolution preview turns it on for every screenshot.
- Deck cards stack in the screen corner, the newest in front with older ones peeking out behind it and a count; hovering the stack fans it out into a column to reach any card, and moving away folds it back (at once with Reduce Motion). Cards show the capture inside your default look, and saved captures reopen in the editor with that look still editable. `easyshot deck toggle` hides and shows the deck. Cards drag into file managers as a copy, and a card that auto-save has saved offers Show in Folder in place of Save.
- Settings › Overlay › Card Actions chooses which actions a preview card shows (Copy, Save / Show in Folder, Edit, Pin, Cloud Share, which opens a panel saying cloud sharing is coming soon, Preview, Delete); Preview opens the capture full size in a plain window (Z or a click toggles 100%, Space or Esc closes it) and Delete moves it to the Trash with an Undo button on the toast; Dismiss is always there and at least one other action stays on.
- Screen recording, one at a time: the tray's Record item becomes Stop Recording while one runs, and system sound is recorded unless you turn it off; with the microphone on too, they are two separate audio tracks, system sound first. The capture bar's recording panel starts with your Recording settings (microphone, system sound, start delay), and a recording that cannot start because a sound device or the area is missing says so.
- A window recording records that window's own pictures where the compositor allows it, so the video shows the window even when it is covered or on another workspace, keeps recording when the window moves to another workspace or monitor, and a window that closes or can no longer be captured ends the recording with the file kept; elsewhere it records the window's area on screen.
- The recording panel lists every open window grouped by workspace, with the ones on screen first, each by its app's icon, title and app name; choosing a window on another workspace switches to it before recording starts, and says so if it cannot.
- The recording control bar can be dragged by its grip anywhere on its screen, and remembers its spot for each screen; it has a mute button for each sound being recorded (system sound, microphone) and tooltips, and it springs in from the capture bar.
- Recording a whole screen puts the control bar on another monitor, so it is not in the video; with one monitor it sits in the bottom-right corner and is recorded. A switch in Settings › Recording turns the bar off, and the tray controls the recording either way.
- With Keystrokes on, each recording saves your clicks and shortcuts (never plain typing) to `input.json` beside the video, and reads nothing while your screen is locked where easyshot can see the lock; it needs your user in the `input` group; the Settings row shows whether it is working, and Settings, the start of a recording and `easyshot doctor` say how to allow it.
- Without a screen recorder installed, the tray's Record item is disabled with an install hint and every other way to start a recording answers with it at once; OCR reads QR codes without Tesseract and says when text needs it.
- Saved recordings and trim exports go to `~/Videos/easyshot/` with dated names, and each save notice says where.
- The trim viewer exports MP4, WebM or GIF, at the original size or scaled down to 1080p, 720p or 480p, in High, Balanced or Small quality, with a brightness slider and four colour looks (Natural, Vivid, Muted, Mono) that you can preview in the player. A recording with system sound and a microphone track has a volume slider for each, system sound starting at 50%; the player plays both and the export mixes them into one track. Your last choices are remembered.
- Open in Studio in the trim viewer and in a video's gallery menu opens a panel saying Video Studio is coming soon.
- The Home window: the media gallery of your captures and recordings (saved, unsaved and trim exports, each with a first-frame thumbnail, opening in the trim viewer) and Settings, one tab each. Settings has Home, General, Capture, Overlay, Recording, Shortcuts, Agents and About, saved as you change them. "System" theme and Reduce Motion follow your desktop.
- The gallery's preview size, from small to two previews a row, set with a slider in its toolbar, Ctrl+scroll over the grid, or Ctrl+plus, Ctrl+minus and Ctrl+0, and remembered in `[gallery] item_size`.
- Settings > Shortcuts and a first-run "Set your shortcuts" step let you choose your own global keys (none are set by default; Print keys are suggested where free). easyshot keeps them in its own binds file for Hyprland, sway or niri, shows the one include line to add to your compositor config with Copy and an Included / Not found check, offers Reload on sway, and checks which keys are already bound. Other desktops use the global-shortcuts portal instead.
- Edits you make by hand to `config.toml` apply while easyshot runs, and a later change in Settings keeps them; a file with a syntax error is left alone and the last good settings stay in use, and a toast names any key easyshot does not know.
- Toasts stay 2.5 seconds, an error 5 and one with Undo 8, and wait while the pointer is on them.
- The Home window slides between Media Gallery and Settings, with the tab selection gliding across; a Settings page rises into place as the last one fades out, and the introduction's steps slide past each other as you move between them; with Reduce Motion they appear at once. The countdown springs in and each second rolls down into place, a new deck card springs into the corner, growing and rising into place as it fades in, and a toast springs in the same way and shrinks away as it fades (with Reduce Motion both only fade). In the editor Saved and Copied fade in and out, a crop grows into the new view, crop Reset and a new aspect ease the crop frame into place, Cancel widens it to the whole image as it fades, a first background springs its padding and shadow in, and a preset, a canvas shape or an alignment draws its way into place; the Settings home tiles fill in softly under the pointer. The blur Direction row, the low-resolution notice and a preset's delete button ease in and out, and the focus pad's knob grows as you drag it and glides home on a double-click. The swatches of a newly picked fill library fade in, a deck card's picture and its "Saving…" label fade in and out, and a deck card's tools and a pin's close button fade in and out with the pointer. Tooltips open after the pointer rests on a control for 1.5 seconds, the next control included, so they never jump from control to control, and hide by themselves once there has been time to read them (2 seconds plus 60 ms a character, at most 8 seconds).
- `easyshot agent` (also `easyshot ai`) for AI agents and scripts: `agent list outputs`, `agent list windows`, `agent capture` (an output, an area, a window or the focused output, saved to the library or to a file you name, with secrets such as keys and passwords pixelated when Tesseract is installed) and `agent ocr` (the text and QR codes on a target or in a PNG or JPEG file), with `--json` printing one JSON object, a distinct exit code for each kind of error and, where the cause is known, an `error.hint` that tells you in one sentence what to do. Agent access is off until you run `easyshot agent enable`; `agent status` and `agent config` show and change consent (ask once per session by default), the outputs and windows agents may capture, and the log of agent actions, and only your own user is answered.
- A consent dialog for agent requests (allow for 1 hour, until logout or always, or deny), a confirmation before `easyshot agent enable` or a looser agent setting takes effect, and Settings › Agents with the access switch, consent, scope, hiding secrets, the active grant with Revoke and the recent agent actions. While a grant is active the tray icon carries a badge and the tray menu offers a one-click Revoke.
- `easyshot agent annotate IMAGE --ops FILE --out PATH` (also under `ai`, and as the `annotate` tool of `agent mcp`) lets an agent draw arrows, boxes, ellipses, text, numbered badges, highlights, blur and pixelation, and an optional background, on an image from a JSON file of operations, with the editor's renderer and your saved annotation style, and write a PNG; it works without the daemon and without agent access.
- `easyshot agent mcp` is a Model Context Protocol server for AI agents with tools to list outputs and windows, capture (returning a scaled-down image of the screenshot), read text, record and export, going through the same agent access, consent and scope as `easyshot agent`, with setup for Claude Code, Codex, Gemini CLI and Cursor in the manual.
- `easyshot agent skill install` puts a skill for AI agents in `~/.agents/skills/easyshot/` (read by Codex, Gemini CLI and Cursor) and links it for Claude Code, or into a project with `--project`; it teaches an agent to check that access is on and ask you when it is not, capture, read text, record and export a demo, and treat what it reads on screen as data, and `agent skill show` and `uninstall` print and remove it.
- `easyshot agent record start|stop|status|discard` lets an agent record an output, an area, a window (switching to its workspace first) or the focused output with the recording bar showing, with `--audio`, `--fps`, `--keystrokes` and a `--max-seconds` limit (120 by default, 600 at most); `stop` prints the video's path, and a recording also stops when agent access ends.
- `easyshot agent export VIDEO` (also under `ai`) re-encodes a recording as MP4, WebM or GIF with the trim viewer's size, quality, look and brightness options and a `--trim A..B` range, printing the new file or, with `--json`, its path, size, duration and bytes.
- The `easyshot` command line: capture, record, OCR, settings and more, `easyshot doctor` with a plain summary, `--raw`, `--json` and `--bundle`, and shell completions for bash, zsh and fish that follow what you have typed: modes, flags, settings pages, `easyshot://` links, image and video files and the open windows for `--window`.
- Notifications you can click: open the file, show it in its folder, or edit a capture.
- The desktop portal is followed while easyshot runs: a file chooser, light or dark and Reduce Motion that start after easyshot are used from then on, an export with no file chooser shows a notice saying where the file went, and `easyshot doctor` reports notification buttons and portal features as they are now.
- Desktop entry (Easyshot in your application menu, like a bare `easyshot`, opens the Home window), systemd user service and D-Bus activation. The Arch package enables the service for every user, though never for a display manager's login screen, and starts it for logged-in users on install; an update restarts it where it is running.
- Starting at login leaves easyshot in the tray: it waits for your panel's tray, adds its icon when the panel appears and again if the panel restarts, and opens the capture bar only if no tray has appeared 10 seconds after it started. The tray names itself easyshot.
- The tray menu shows your global shortcuts beside its items, lists your recent screenshots and recordings, saved or not, and offers Unpin All Windows while a screenshot is pinned. A capture started from the menu waits for your panel to close the menu, so the menu is never in the shot.
- `curl -fsSL https://easyshot.site/install.sh | sh` installs the latest release on any distribution after checking its checksum and signature, system-wide or just for you (`--user`), starts it, and removes it again with `--uninstall`.
- The release tarball has an `install.sh` that installs easyshot for all users or just you (`--user`) on any distribution, starts it, and removes it again with `--uninstall`.
